## Abstract

Point clouds represent 3D object geometry as sets of spatial coordinates. A
fundamental task in their analysis is keypoint detection, i.e., identifying a
sparse subset of points that captures the most salient features. While
supervised methods rely on manual annotations, unsupervised approaches leverage
intrinsic geometric properties to localize keypoints without human input. We
introduce GEKey, a self-supervised framework that uses the geodesic eccentricity
transform, an SE(3)-invariant geometric measure, to identify instance-specific
salient points. These points serve as pseudo-labels to train a model that aligns
its predictions with the object's intrinsic structure. GEKey is simple,
efficient, and robust, preserving intra-class variation by balancing
instance-specific and category-level structure. Extensive experiments on
ShapeNetCoreV2, KeypointNet, CAPE, ITOP, and DeformingThings4D show
state-of-the-art performance in both keypoint detection and alignment, with
strong resilience to noise, downsampling, deformations, and real-world
conditions.
